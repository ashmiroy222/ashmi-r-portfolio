MIC: CASA Platform Redesign

Redesigning how Court Appointed Special Advocates write court reports and act on flagged case content, so volunteers spend less time on paperwork and more time on the child.

Overview

MIC is a cloud-based platform that helps CASA volunteers manage cases and write court reports.

MIC helps Court Appointed Special Advocates (CASAs) handle clerical work, so they can focus on children in CHINS cases: kids who need services because of neglect, abuse, or unsafe situations. CASA volunteers write the court reports that help judges decide what is best for a child.

Our team of nine set out to make report writing and case management faster. I owned user testing and the testing protocol, took the homepage and document page from sketch to final prototype, and organized our process.

Design question

How might we design intuitive user flows that ensure efficiency for the user?

Who we designed for

CASA volunteers

Our primary users. They use MIC daily to track cases and write reports.

Everyone around the case

Judges, case managers, the child, and their families.

Our brief: refine the MIC platform to cut the time volunteers spend on clerical work in report writing and case management.

The final deliverable was a high-fidelity, interactive Figma prototype with two flows, handed to the sponsor. We worked in three milestones: understand the platform, design the user flows, then build the interactive prototype.

How it worked
  • 01Up to 30 minutes each
  • 023 CASAs, from 6 months to 10 years of experience
  • 03Questions for each page, compared with Optima, the system they already use

What we heard

  • Voice-to-text and picture uploads were liked. Drafts and privacy settings were wanted.
  • Red and green buttons were confusing and hard for colorblind users.

What it changed

  • Save as Draft next to Submit.
  • Color-only buttons flagged as an accessibility fix.

Outcome

What we worked within

  • No legal or compliance experts to check AI use.
  • Limited firsthand view of the full CASA workflow.
  • Testing with a small number of CASA volunteers.

Recommended next

  • Create a report from the case view.
  • More interactivity, like rearranging cards.
  • Onboarding tooltips, and testing with more CASAs.

Design Approach

Prototyped in Figma and mapped in FigJam, with usability heuristics and UX laws in mind. Every AI suggestion shows a disclaimer and can be skipped, edited, or confirmed.

Figma FigJam Usability Testing Cognitive Walkthrough
Reflection
In a sensitive setting like child welfare, an AI feature has to earn trust before it can save time. Letting volunteers skip, edit, and confirm every suggestion mattered as much as the suggestion itself.
